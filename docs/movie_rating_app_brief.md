# 8mm: the brief

## The idea

A star rating is a single number standing in for a lot of separate judgments. When I pick that number on gut feel, it drifts with my mood, and two very different films end up tied. 8mm splits the judgment into eight smaller questions that are easy to answer honestly, then does the math.

The name works two ways. There are eight criteria, and 8mm is a film format. The tagline is "Eight perspectives. One rating."

## What it should feel like

It should feel like a well-made calculator, not a film database or a social app. You open it after the credits, tap through eight rows in under a minute, copy the rating, and close it. The look follows the same idea: warm off-white paper, charcoal type, a little amber for the stars, and plenty of space. A small film magazine more than a dashboard.

## The eight criteria

Story, Direction, Acting, Pacing, Depth, Cinematography, Screenplay, and Emotional Impact.

Some of these overlap on purpose, and the pairs are worth spelling out:

- **Story and Screenplay.** A great premise doesn't guarantee good dialogue or well-built scenes.
- **Depth and Emotional Impact.** A film can be clever without being moving, and a simple film can still hit hard.
- **Depth is optional for greatness.** A good comedy or action film can score a 0 on Depth and still rate well overall.

Sound, music, and originality came up early on. I left them out on purpose. Eight criteria is the limit.

## How scoring works

Each criterion gets 0, ½, or 1, and all eight count equally. The total, out of 8, scales to five stars and rounds to the nearest half star, because that's what Letterboxd uses. I picked three options per criterion over a finer 1-5 scale because a coarse answer is faster to give and harder to fudge.

Every criterion starts unscored, and an unscored row is different from a 0. The rating appears only once all eight are scored, so the app never shows a half-finished number as if it were final. An all-zero result shows 0.5★, the lowest rating Letterboxd allows.

## What it won't do

8mm has no accounts, no history, no movie search, and no Letterboxd integration. Letterboxd already does all of that well. Any new feature has to fix a problem I've hit while using the app, not one I can imagine hitting.
